AI Policy Analysis

Analyzing per-pupil expenditure and NAEP 4th-grade reading performance across all 50 states and Washington D.C., a striking paradox emerges: the states spending the most per student consistently rank at the bottom for spending efficiency. Utah spends $8,154 per student and achieves 39% reading proficiency — 47.8 proficiency points per $10,000 spent. New York spends $26,571 and achieves 35%, yielding just 13.2 points per $10,000. That is a 3.6× efficiency gap for less learning. The pattern holds across the ten highest-spending states: eight of them fall in the bottom half of the efficiency ranking. What separates high-efficiency states? Florida, Mississippi, Idaho, and Utah share a policy thread: early-reading mandates backed by structured literacy curricula, K–3 reading coaches, and in some states mandatory third-grade retention for students who cannot read at grade level — interventions that do not require record spending to implement. Tennessee ranks 16th nationally in efficiency with $11,200 per student and a 33% proficiency rate, placing it in the top third and on an improving trajectory since the 2021 Tennessee Literacy Success Act. The data does not argue for cutting education budgets — it argues that how money is spent determines outcomes far more than how much.

What High-Efficiency States Have in Common

AI pattern analysis across the top 10 most efficient states reveals four consistent policy threads.

📖
Structured Literacy Mandates
Utah, Idaho, Mississippi, and Florida all passed laws requiring phonics-based, evidence-based reading instruction in K–3 classrooms — before top-spending states did.
👩‍🏫
K–3 Reading Coaches
Florida placed certified reading coaches in every elementary school. Mississippi embedded literacy coaches district-wide. Neither required record per-pupil budgets to do so.
📋
Early Intervention Systems
Iowa, Indiana, and North Carolina use early-warning data to identify struggling readers in kindergarten and first grade — and intervene before deficits compound.
💸
High Spending ≠ High Outcomes
D.C., New York, Alaska, Connecticut, and Hawaii are in the bottom 10 for efficiency. High union costs, administrative overhead, and pension obligations absorb spending before it reaches classrooms.
